# Browser workspace investigation — September 5, 2026

The owner reports that repeated `Super + Shift + Left/Right` Browser moves make the controls and surrounding frame disappear while the webpage remains. The shell becomes very slow, can display recovery, and recovers either after waiting or after closing the affected Browser. Up/Down monitor moves were also present in the reproduction. This report reopens the previously deferred laptop investigation.

## Evidence and scope

- The initial observed installed and running release was `1.0.13`. The shell and its bootstrap retained their original process identities during that reproduction; no agent restart occurred.
- The installed log around 14:38–14:39 records repeated transitions with zero animation frames and approximately 2,050 ms completion delays. A UI responsiveness probe was delayed 43,860 ms. Subsequent moves after the affected Browser closed returned to approximately 300 ms.
- The log does not identify the precise blocking native/COM call. The owner saw recovery; no matching managed exception stack was found. A hybrid graphics configuration is a possible contributing factor, not an established cause.
- `TryCaptureTransientOpacity` captured the Browser's WPF content root and the workspace fade reduced that root to zero. WebView2 is a native child `HwndHost`; WPF root opacity does not include its pixels. This establishes the disappearing-chrome defect independently of the longer stall. [Microsoft's WPF interoperation documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/desktop/wpf/advanced/wpf-and-win32-interoperation) documents that limitation.

## Source candidate

`IStageWindowSurface` declares native child content before asynchronous initialization. Browser and Terminal use that declaration; the shared visual policy also detects other native hosts in logical and visual descendants. Opening and transition capture decline WPF-only fades for those windows. They retain their live controls and native content while the established workspace geometry moves the complete window. Pure WPF fades and whole-window desktop dimming retain their existing behavior. No WebView2 runtime flags, graphics preferences, drivers, settings schema, keyboard mapping or recovery policy changed.

`UiResponsivenessMonitor` can optionally record operations that complete after 250 ms. The candidate names inactive-window hiding, individual native focus steps and Browser memory-target updates, without including page URLs or window titles. These are diagnostics for the unresolved stall, not a claim that the blocking call has been found.

## Follow-up after installing Preview `20260905-183248`

The owner reports that the problem remains after updating. The installed record and actual shell/bootstrap executable paths now match `20260905-183248`. The new named timing records prove that the instrumentation is running: around 15:36, `Workspace focus: bring to top` and `Workspace focus: keyboard focus` each take approximately 2,000 ms, `Workspace visibility: hide inactive window` takes approximately 4,000 ms, and the dispatcher reports a 28,050 ms delay. No Browser memory-target stall was identified in that sequence. The exact slow native child handler and any hardware contribution remain unproven.

The local follow-up corrects the focus and visibility lifecycle:

- Activation previously attached the shell input queue to other threads, restored/showed the window, brought it to the top, and forced keyboard focus onto the top-level HWND even when its native child already held focus. The candidate keeps that child focus, skips already-foreground activation, and uses the existing bounded foreground handoff without input-queue attachment. Cross-thread show/restore requests use `ShowWindowAsync`. Microsoft's [input-queue explanation](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/oldnewthing/20130607-00/?p=4143) describes why attached queues and focus changes can block synchronously. The laptop timing records identify the affected calls; this platform explanation does not establish which internal WebView2 handler was waiting.
- Directional, adjacent, direct and Floating follow commands now refresh inventory before reassigning the app, and skip the intermediate refresh before entering the destination workspace. That intermediate scan previously treated the travelling app as leaked inactive content and hid it. Cross-monitor moves also defer source reflow until the destination transition settles, avoiding the same scan through tiling. Ordinary workspace leave/return retains its hide/restore behavior.
- Normal tiled geometry repair no longer calls activating `SW_RESTORE` on every sibling. It restores only minimized or maximized windows that actually require it; ordinary geometry changes retain `SWP_NOACTIVATE`.

The added private-desktop regression first ran against the previous production behavior: 9 of 59 checks fail. All eight Browser move visibility checks see a hide, and the native child fixture loses focus and enters its bounded 300 ms focus-loss handler (318 ms activation elapsed). The first corrected candidate passes all 59; the same focus fixture receives zero focus-loss callbacks and records 0 ms activation. This fixture models the observed synchronous wait and does not claim to reproduce the installed 28-second delay.

Expanded checks caught the extra pre-transition cross-monitor tiling scan; after correcting it, all 117 Browser/Terminal/Text/Files checks pass. They include sibling tiling focus, direct and adjacent commands, both connected monitor destinations, Tiled/Floating, Reduced Motion, interruptions, leave/return, and Light/Dark render checks. Release builds pass with warnings as errors; the final quiet self-test passes all 144 checks. Local evidence is under `artifacts/browser-focus-before-20260905`, `browser-focus-after-20260905`, `browser-focus-expanded-20260905` and `browser-focus-final-20260905`.

The wider private desktop suite passes 555 of 556 checks. Its embedded-player play/pause pointer probe reports `clicks=0` on both the final candidate and a separately archived/built unchanged `701d2e4` source baseline. The same failure also occurred in the first candidate run. All workspace, monitor, focus, maximize/restore and other checks in those reports pass. This pre-existing private-desktop media input limit remains visible; the wider suite is not reported as wholly passing. A bounded read-only Windows wait-chain capture records 77 samples of the shell UI thread blocked on cross-process `SendMessage`. Receiver processes exited before identification; a subsequent managed-stack capture window saw no reproduction. The exact stalled call and receiver identity remain unconfirmed. These observations do not establish a GPU or driver fault.

The candidate developed on `codex/browser-close-latency` corrects two independently observable lifecycle defects:

- Browser previously disposed WebView2 only from `Closed`, after native owner destruction had begun. Individual tab close/recreation removed the control from its visual parent before disposal. WPF's [HwndHost source](https://github.com/dotnet/wpf/blob/main/src/Microsoft.DotNet.Wpf/src/PresentationFramework/System/Windows/Interop/HwndHost.cs) reparents an undisposed child to its hidden notification window when its presentation source disappears. The candidate disposes on the owning UI thread after all `Closing` subscribers can cancel, before owner teardown, and disposes individual tab views before visual detachment. Cleanup is idempotent with an `OnClosed` fallback; pending initialization cannot reattach a retired view. This removes an unnecessary native reparent path, but remains a candidate for the observed multi-second wait until live acceptance.
- `BeginPendingCloseFade` still faded the WPF content root without applying the native-content exclusion used by opening/movement. It now uses the same shared policy, preserving Browser/Terminal controls with their hosted content while closing. Pure WPF close animation remains available.

The private `browser-close` acceptance runs before normal app fixtures and covers a cold renderer, repeated direct/shell closes, cancellation, multiple tabs, tab detachment, owner destruction, pending initialization, Tiled/Floating, Reduced Motion and activation of the surviving app. The original source fails the lifecycle/chrome assertions; the candidate passes all 45 final checks. Final private close timings are 30–325 ms. Private activation uses the thread's active window, following the existing desktop acceptance convention; the global input desktop is never switched. Initial test-only probes were corrected to handle the WebView2 SDK throwing `ObjectDisposedException` from disposed getters and to avoid using global foreground state for private-desktop activation.
